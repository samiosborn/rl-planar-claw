# Policy Gradients

## Setup

**Definition — Markov Decision Process**

A Markov decision process is a tuple

$$
\mathcal{M}
=
(\mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, P, r, \rho_0, \gamma)
$$

where

* $\mathcal{S}$ is the state space;
* $\mathcal{A}$ is the action space;
* $P(s' \mid s,a)$ is the transition probability;
* $r(s,a)$ is the reward function;
* $\rho_0(s)$ is the initial-state distribution;
* $\gamma \in [0,1]$ is the discount factor.

---

**Definition — Policy**

A stochastic policy parameterised by $\theta$ is a probability distribution over actions conditioned on the current state:

$$
\pi_\theta(a \mid s)
=
P(a_t = a \mid s_t = s; \theta)
$$

---

**Definition — Trajectory**

A trajectory is a sequence of states and actions generated by interaction between the policy and the environment:

$$
\tau
=
(s_0, a_0, s_1, a_1, \ldots, s_T)
$$

Under the policy $\pi_\theta$, trajectories are sampled according to

$$
\tau \sim p_\theta(\tau)
$$

---

**Definition — Trajectory Probability**

The probability of a trajectory under policy $\pi_\theta$ is

$$
p_\theta(\tau)
=
\rho_0(s_0)
\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}
\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)
P(s_{t+1} \mid s_t,a_t)
$$

The dependence on $\theta$ comes only from the policy $\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)$.

The environment dynamics and initial-state distribution $\rho_0(s_0)$ do not depend on the policy parameters.

---

**Definition — Return**

The discounted return of a trajectory is

$$
R(\tau)
=
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
\gamma^t r_t
$$

---

**Definition — Reward-to-Go**

The reward-to-go from time $t$ is

$$
G_t
=
\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}
\gamma^{k-t} r_k
$$

---

**Definition — Policy Objective**

The objective of the policy is to maximise expected return:

$$
J(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim p_\theta(\tau)}
\left[
R(\tau)
\right]
$$

Equivalently,

$$
J(\theta)
=
\int
p_\theta(\tau)
R(\tau)
\, d\tau
$$

Policy-gradient methods therefore seek to compute the policy gradient

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
$$

## Optimising the Policy Objective

**Definition — Score Function**

The score function is the gradient of the log-probability of an action with respect to the policy parameters $\theta$:

$$
\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)
$$

---

### Log-Derivative Identity

Consider the derivative of the logarithm,

$$
\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(x)
=
\frac{\nabla_\theta p_\theta(x)}{p_\theta(x)}
$$

So

$$
\nabla_\theta p_\theta(x)
=
p_\theta(x)
\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(x)
$$

This is called the log-derivative trick, likelihood-ratio trick, or score-function trick.

### Differentiating the Policy Objective

For the policy objective,

$$
J(\theta)
=
\int p_\theta(\tau) R(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Differentiate with respect to $\theta$:

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
=
\nabla_\theta 
\int
p_\theta(\tau)
R(\tau)\,d\tau
=
\int
\nabla_\theta p_\theta(\tau)
R(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Applying the log-derivative trick gives

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
=
\int
p_\theta(\tau)
\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(\tau)
R(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Recognising this integral as an expectation,

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim p_\theta}
\left[
R(\tau)
\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(\tau)
\right]
$$

The log-derivative trick converts the gradient of a probability distribution into an expectation over samples from that distribution. This allows the gradient to be estimated using sampled trajectories.

## Trajectory Policy Gradient

From the previous section,

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim p_\theta}
\left[
R(\tau)
\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(\tau)
\right]
$$

The trajectory probability is

$$
p_\theta(\tau)
=
\rho_0(s_0)
\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}
\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)
P(s_{t+1} \mid s_t,a_t)
$$

Taking the logarithm converts the product into a sum:

$$
\log p_\theta(\tau)
=
\log \rho_0(s_0)
+
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
\log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)
+
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
\log P(s_{t+1} \mid s_t,a_t)
$$

Differentiating with respect to $\theta$ gives

$$
\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(\tau)
=
\nabla_\theta \log \rho_0(s_0)
+
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)
+
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
\nabla_\theta \log P(s_{t+1} \mid s_t,a_t)
$$

The initial-state distribution and environment dynamics do not depend on $\theta$, so

$$
\nabla_\theta \log \rho_0(s_0) = 0
$$

And

$$
\nabla_\theta \log P(s_{t+1} \mid s_t,a_t) = 0
$$

Therefore,

$$
\nabla_\theta \log p_\theta(\tau)
=
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
\nabla_\theta
\log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)
$$

Substituting this into the gradient of the objective gives

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim p_\theta}
\left[
R(\tau)
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
\nabla_\theta
\log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)
\right]
$$

Given

$$
R(\tau)
=
\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}
\gamma^k r_k
$$

We can write

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)
\sum_{k=0}^{T-1}
\gamma^k r_k
\right]
$$

For a given action $a_t$, rewards received before time $t$ cannot have been caused by that action.

The contribution of these earlier rewards has zero expectation:

$$
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)
\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}
\gamma^k r_k
\right]
=
0
$$

Therefore, each policy-gradient term only needs to be weighted by rewards obtained from time $t$ onward.

Using the reward-to-go

$$
G_t
=
\sum_{k=t}^{T-1}
\gamma^{k-t} r_k
$$

The reward-to-go form of the policy gradient is

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
G_t
\nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)
\right]
$$

## Policy Gradient Theorem

**Definition — Action-Value (Q-Function)**

$$
Q^\pi(s,a)
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
G_t
\mid
s_t=s,\,
a_t=a
\right]
$$

It gives the expected future return after taking action $a$ in state $s$ and then continuing to follow policy $\pi$.

---

Since $Q^\pi(s_t,a_t)$ is the conditional expectation of $G_t$, we can replace the sampled return-to-go with its expected value:

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
Q^\pi(s_t,a_t)
\nabla_\theta
\log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)
\right]
$$

---

**Definition — Discounted State-visitation Distribution**

$$
d^\pi(s)
=
(1-\gamma)
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}
\gamma^t
P(s_t=s \mid \pi)
$$

It represents the expected chance of visiting a state within a trajectory following the policy, with future visits discounted by $\gamma$.

---

Then the policy gradient theorem can be written as

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\mathbb{E}_{
s \sim d^\pi,\,
a \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid s)
}
\left[
Q^\pi(s,a)
\nabla_\theta
\log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)
\right]
$$

The factor $1/(1-\gamma)$ depends on the convention used to define $d^\pi(s)$. If the state-visitation measure is left unnormalised, this factor is absorbed into the distribution.

The important result is therefore

$$
\boxed{
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
\propto
\mathbb{E}
\left[
Q^\pi(s,a)
\nabla_\theta
\log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)
\right]
}
$$

The theorem shows that the gradient of expected return can be computed from states and actions sampled from the current policy, weighted by how valuable those actions are.

A key consequence is that we do not need to explicitly differentiate the state-visitation distribution $d^\pi(s)$ with respect to $\theta$.

## Baselines and Advantage

Consider the expected score of the policy

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi_\theta}
\left[
\nabla_\theta
\log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)
\right]
&=
\sum_a
\pi_\theta(a \mid s)
\nabla_\theta
\log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)
\\
&=
\sum_a
\nabla_\theta
\pi_\theta(a \mid s)
\\
&=
\nabla_\theta
\sum_a
\pi_\theta(a \mid s)
\\
&=
\nabla_\theta 1
\\
&=
0
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, for any function $b(s)$, 

$$
\mathbb{E}
\left[
b(s)
\nabla_\theta
\log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)
\right]
=
0
$$

Hence, 

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\left(
Q^\pi(s,a)-b(s)
\right)
\nabla_\theta
\log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)
\right]
$$

The function $b(s)$ is called a baseline. Its purpose is to reduce the variance of the gradient estimate without changing its expectation.

A common choice is the state-value function

$$
V^\pi(s)
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
G_t \mid s_t=s
\right]
$$

---

**Definition — Advantage Function**

The advantage function measures how much better or worse it is to take action $a$ in state $s$ compared with the policy's expected value of being in that state:

$$
A^\pi(s,a)
=
Q^\pi(s,a)
-
V^\pi(s)
$$

---

Using $V^\pi(s)$ as the baseline gives the advantage form of the policy gradient:

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}
\left[
A^\pi(s,a)
\nabla_\theta
\log \pi_\theta(a \mid s)
\right]
$$

This form is especially important because later actor-critic methods, GAE, TRPO and PPO all rely on estimating the advantage rather than using raw returns directly.

## Monte Carlo Gradient Estimate

The policy gradient is an expectation, so in practice it is estimated using sampled trajectories:

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
\approx
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\sum_t
G_t^{(i)}
\nabla_\theta
\log
\pi_\theta(a_t^{(i)} \mid s_t^{(i)}).
$$

This sampled estimate forms the basis of the REINFORCE algorithm.
