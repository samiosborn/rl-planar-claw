# Proximal Policy Optimisation

## Why Policy Updates Need to Be Constrained

Suppose the current policy has parameters $\theta_{\mathrm{old}}$. The policy-gradient theorem gives the gradient of the expected return at this policy:

$$
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
\bigg|_{\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}
=
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}
\left[
A^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}(S_t,A_t)
\nabla_\theta
\log
\pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t)
\bigg|_{\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}
\right]
$$

This gradient gives the local direction of increasing expected return.

A basic policy-gradient update is

$$
\theta
\leftarrow
\theta_{\mathrm{old}}
+
\alpha
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
\bigg|_{\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}
$$

The difficulty is that the gradient contains only local information about $J$ around $\theta_{\mathrm{old}}$.

Using a first-order Taylor approximation,

$$
J(\theta)
\approx
J(\theta_{\mathrm{old}})
+
\nabla_\theta J(\theta)
\bigg|_{\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}^{\top}
(\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{old}})
$$

This approximation becomes less reliable as $\theta$ moves further from $\theta_{\mathrm{old}}$.

A sufficiently large update can therefore change the policy substantially even if the gradient at $\theta_{\mathrm{old}}$ pointed in a useful direction.

---

### Parameter Distance vs. Policy Distance

Constraining the parameter change directly, does not in general constrain how much the action distribution changes.

$$
\left\|
\theta-\theta_{\mathrm{old}}
\right\|
$$

This mapping is nonlinear and depends on the parameterisation of the policy. 

$$
\theta
\mapsto
\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s)
$$

A small parameter change can produce a large change in action probabilities.

What matters for policy optimisation is therefore not primarily how far the parameters move, but how far the resulting probability distributions move:

$$
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid s)
\longrightarrow
\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s)
$$

This motivates methods that restrict or penalise large changes in the policy itself rather than simply choosing a small parameter-space learning rate.

---

### Old Policy vs. New Policy

Policy optimisation also introduces a second problem.

A batch of trajectories is generated using the rollout policy $ \pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}} $

So the observed actions satisfy

$$
A_t
\sim
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid S_t)
$$

After an optimisation step, however, the policy becomes $ \pi_\theta $ - so the existing samples were not generated by this new policy.

Consequently, once $\theta\neq\theta_{\mathrm{old}}$, expectations over the collected batch are expectations under $\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}$, not under $\pi_\theta$.

---

## The Surrogate Policy Objective

The central problem is therefore to construct an objective for $\pi_\theta$ using samples generated by $\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}$.

### Importance Sampling

**Theorem — Change of Measure**

Let $p$ and $q$ be probability distributions such that $p(x)>0$ whenever $q(x)>0$. Then

$$
\mathbb{E}_{X\sim q}
\left[
f(X)
\right]
=
\mathbb{E}_{X\sim p}
\left[
\frac{q(X)}{p(X)}
f(X)
\right]
$$

**Proof**

To see this in the discrete case,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{X\sim p}
\left[
\frac{q(X)}{p(X)}
f(X)
\right]
&=
\sum_x
p(x)
\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}
f(x)
\\
&=
\sum_x
q(x)f(x)
\\
&=
\mathbb{E}_{X\sim q}
\left[
f(X)
\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

Applied to actions at a fixed state $s$,

$$
\mathbb{E}_{A\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s)}
\left[
f(A)
\right]
=
\mathbb{E}_{A\sim\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid s)}
\left[
\frac{
\pi_\theta(A\mid s)
}{
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(A\mid s)
}
f(A)
\right]
$$

This motivates the probability ratio

$$
r_t(\theta)
=
\frac{
\pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t)
}{
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(A_t\mid S_t)
}
$$

The ratio corrects for the change in the conditional action distribution. It does not correct for changes in the distribution of visited states.

---

### Performance Difference Lemma

Define the discounted state-visitation distribution under policy $\pi$ by

$$
d^\pi(s)
=
(1-\gamma)
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}
\gamma^t
\Pr_\pi(S_t=s)
$$

**Theorem — Performance Difference Lemma**

For two policies $\pi$ and $\pi'$,

$$
J(\pi')
-
J(\pi)
=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\mathbb{E}_{
S\sim d^{\pi'},
A\sim\pi'(\cdot\mid S)
}
\left[
A^\pi(S,A)
\right]
$$

**Proof**

To derive this, first note that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{A_t\sim\pi'}
\left[
A^\pi(S_t,A_t)
\right]
&=
\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}
\left[
R_{t+1}
+
\gamma V^\pi(S_{t+1})
-
V^\pi(S_t)
\mid
S_t
\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

Taking expectations over trajectories generated by $\pi'$ and summing with discounting, assuming the initial-state distribution is the same under both policies,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}
\gamma^t
\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}
\left[
A^\pi(S_t,A_t)
\right]
&=
\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}
\left[
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}
\gamma^t
\left(
R_{t+1}
+
\gamma V^\pi(S_{t+1})
-
V^\pi(S_t)
\right)
\right]
\\
&=
\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}
\left[
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}
\gamma^tR_{t+1}
+
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}
\gamma^{t+1}V^\pi(S_{t+1})
-
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}
\gamma^tV^\pi(S_t)
\right]
\\
&=
\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}
\left[
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}
\gamma^tR_{t+1}
-
V^\pi(S_0)
\right]
\\
&=
J(\pi')
-
J(\pi)
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the definition of $d^{\pi'}$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}_{
S\sim d^{\pi'},
A\sim\pi'
}
\left[
A^\pi(S,A)
\right]
&=
\sum_s
d^{\pi'}(s)
\sum_a
\pi'(a\mid s)
A^\pi(s,a)
\\
&=
(1-\gamma)
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}
\gamma^t
\sum_s
\Pr_{\pi'}(S_t=s)
\sum_a
\pi'(a\mid s)
A^\pi(s,a)
\\
&=
(1-\gamma)
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}
\gamma^t
\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}
\left[
A^\pi(S_t,A_t)
\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}
\gamma^t
\mathbb{E}_{\pi'}
\left[
A^\pi(S_t,A_t)
\right]
=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\mathbb{E}_{
S\sim d^{\pi'},
A\sim\pi'
}
\left[
A^\pi(S,A)
\right]
$$

which gives the result.

---

### Surrogate Objective

The performance difference lemma gives

$$
J(\theta)
-
J(\theta_{\mathrm{old}})
=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\mathbb{E}_{
S\sim d^{\pi_\theta},
A\sim\pi_\theta
}
\left[
A^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}(S,A)
\right]
$$

This expression depends on $ d^{\pi_\theta} $ but the collected trajectories follow $ d^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}} $

For policies close to one another, approximate the new state-visitation distribution by the old one:

$$
d^{\pi_\theta}
\approx
d^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}
$$

This gives the surrogate objective

$$
L_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{
S\sim d^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}},
A\sim\pi_\theta
}
\left[
A^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}(S,A)
\right]
$$

where the factor $1/(1-\gamma)$ and the constant $J(\theta_{\mathrm{old}})$ can be omitted when optimising with respect to $\theta$.

The change from $d^{\pi_\theta}$ to $d^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}$ is an approximation. The remaining change in the action distribution can be handled exactly using importance sampling:

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\theta)
&=
\mathbb{E}_{
S\sim d^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}},
A\sim\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}
}
\left[
\frac{
\pi_\theta(A\mid S)
}{
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(A\mid S)
}
A^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}(S,A)
\right]
\\
&=
\mathbb{E}_{
S,A\sim\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}
}
\left[
r_t(\theta)
A^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}(S_t,A_t)
\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

In practice, the true advantage is replaced by an estimator such as General Advantage Estimation (GAE):

$$
L^{\mathrm{PG}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_t
\left[
r_t(\theta)
\hat A_t
\right]
$$

This is the surrogate policy objective used as the starting point for PPO.

---

### Gradient Agreement at the Old Policy

At the rollout policy,

$$
r_t(\theta_{\mathrm{old}})
=
1
$$

The gradient of the probability ratio is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla_\theta r_t(\theta)
&=
\frac{
\nabla_\theta\pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t)
}{
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(A_t\mid S_t)
}
\\
&=
\frac{
\pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t)
}{
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(A_t\mid S_t)
}
\nabla_\theta
\log\pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t)
\\
&=
r_t(\theta)
\nabla_\theta
\log\pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\nabla_\theta r_t(\theta)
\bigg|_{\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}
=
\nabla_\theta
\log\pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t)
\bigg|_{\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}
$$

For

$$
L^{\mathrm{PG}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_t
\left[
r_t(\theta)\hat A_t
\right]
$$

We treat $\hat A_t$ as fixed during the actor update,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla_\theta L^{\mathrm{PG}}(\theta)
\bigg|_{\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}
&=
\mathbb{E}_t
\left[
\hat A_t
\nabla_\theta r_t(\theta)
\right]_{\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}
\\
&=
\mathbb{E}_t
\left[
\hat A_t
\nabla_\theta
\log\pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t)
\right]_{\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus the surrogate objective has the same first-order gradient as the ordinary policy-gradient objective at $\theta_{\mathrm{old}}$. 

---

## Trust Regions

The surrogate objective agrees with the ordinary policy-gradient objective locally at $\theta_{\mathrm{old}}$.

$$
L_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_t
\left[
r_t(\theta)\hat A_t
\right]
$$

### Why the Surrogate Is Only a Local Approximation

The exact performance difference depends on the new policy's discounted state-visitation distribution:

$$
J(\theta)
-
J(\theta_{\mathrm{old}})
=
\frac{1}{1-\gamma}
\mathbb{E}_{
S\sim d^{\pi_\theta},
A\sim\pi_\theta
}
\left[
A^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}(S,A)
\right]
$$

The surrogate objective replaces $d^{\pi_\theta}$ with $d^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}$, so that $d^{\pi_\theta} \approx d^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}$ is treated as a local approximation.

As $\pi_\theta$ moves further from $\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}$, the two policies can visit increasingly different states, causing a divergence in $d^{\pi_\theta}$ vs. $d^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}$

### Measuring Policy Change with KL Divergence

We compare the action distributions using the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence. 

For a fixed state $s$,

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\left(
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid s)
\|
\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s)
\right)
=
\mathbb{E}_{A\sim\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid s)}
\left[
\log
\frac{
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(A\mid s)
}{
\pi_\theta(A\mid s)
}
\right]
$$

This is zero when the two action distributions are identical.

A state-averaged measure of the policy change is

$$
\mathbb{E}_{
S\sim d^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}
}
\left[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\left(
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid S)
\|
\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid S)
\right)
\right]
$$

This measures how much the action distribution changes over states visited by the rollout policy.

### TRPO as a Constrained Optimisation Problem

Trust Region Policy Optimisation maximises the surrogate objective subject to a constraint on policy change.

Conceptually,

$$
\max_\theta
L_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\theta)
$$

Subject to

$$
\mathbb{E}_{
S\sim d^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}
}
\left[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\left(
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(\cdot\mid S)
\|
\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid S)
\right)
\right]
\leq
\delta
$$

Where $\delta>0$.

## The PPO Clipped Objective

Reminder: The surrogate policy objective is

$$
L^{\mathrm{PG}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_t
\left[
r_t(\theta)\hat A_t
\right]
$$

With the probability ratio

$$
r_t(\theta)
=
\frac{
\pi_\theta(A_t\mid S_t)
}{
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(A_t\mid S_t)
}
$$


### Clipped Surrogate Objective

The PPO clipped objective is

$$
L^{\mathrm{CLIP}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_t
\left[
\min
\left(
r_t(\theta)\hat A_t,
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
r_t(\theta),
1-\epsilon,
1+\epsilon
\right)
\hat A_t
\right)
\right]
$$

The effect of clipping depends on the sign of $\hat A_t$.

### Positive Advantages

Suppose $ \hat A_t>0 $, then the sampled action performed better than the policy's baseline expectation at that state.

Increasing its probability corresponds to $ r_t(\theta)>1 $

For $\hat A_t>0$,

$$
\min
\left(
r_t\hat A_t,
\operatorname{clip}(r_t,1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\hat A_t
\right)
$$

Can be written as

$$
\begin{cases}
r_t\hat A_t,
&
r_t\leq 1+\epsilon
\\
(1+\epsilon)\hat A_t,
&
r_t>1+\epsilon
\end{cases}
$$

Thus, increasing the probability of a positive-advantage action improves the clipped objective only until $r_t=1+\epsilon$.

If $r_t<1-\epsilon$, the probability has instead moved in the unfavourable direction. PPO leaves this term unclipped so that the objective continues to penalise the decrease.

### Negative Advantages

Suppose $ \hat A_t<0 $, then the sampled action performed worse than the policy's baseline expectation.

Decreasing its probability corresponds to $ r_t(\theta)<1 $

The clipped objective becomes

$$
\begin{cases}
(1-\epsilon)\hat A_t,
&
r_t<1-\epsilon
\\
r_t\hat A_t,
&
r_t\geq 1-\epsilon
\end{cases}
$$

Thus decreasing the probability of a negative-advantage action improves the objective only until $ r_t = 1-\epsilon $

Beyond this point, further decreases in the sampled action probability do not improve its contribution to the clipped objective.

When $ r_t>1+\epsilon $, the objective is not clipped. This corresponds to moving in the unfavourable direction by increasing the probability of a negative-advantage action.

### Why the Minimum Appears

Since we maximise the objective, the minimum gives a pessimistic version of the surrogate improvement.

For $\hat A_t>0$, it prevents the objective from benefiting when $ r_t>1+\epsilon $

For $\hat A_t<0$, it prevents the objective from benefiting when $ r_t<1-\epsilon $

The minimum is necessary because clipping the ratio unconditionally would also remove penalties for changes in the wrong direction.

### What Clipping Does Not Guarantee

PPO clipping does not impose the hard constraint

$$
1-\epsilon
\leq
r_t(\theta)
\leq
1+\epsilon
$$

PPO also does not guarantee a fixed bound on

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}
\left(
\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}
\|
\pi_\theta
\right)
$$

Unlike TRPO, it does not impose an explicit trust-region constraint.

## PPO as an Actor-Critic Method

PPO combines a clipped policy objective for the actor with value-function learning for the critic.

A batch is first collected using the fixed rollout policy $ \pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}} $

The resulting trajectories are then used to estimate advantages and optimise both actor and critic.

### GAE in PPO

The critic provides value estimates

$$
V_\phi(S_t)
$$

Which can be used to form TD residuals

$$
\delta_t
=
R_{t+1}
+
\gamma V_\phi(S_{t+1})
-
V_\phi(S_t)
$$

GAE then gives

$$
\hat A_t
=
\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}
(\gamma\lambda)^l
\delta_{t+l}
$$

GAE therefore supplies the estimate of $ A^{\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}}(S_t,A_t) $ that appears in the PPO surrogate objective.

### Actor Objective

The actor maximises the PPO clipped objective

$$
L^{\mathrm{CLIP}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_t
\left[
\min
\left(
r_t(\theta)\hat A_t,
\operatorname{clip}
\left(
r_t(\theta),
1-\epsilon,
1+\epsilon
\right)
\hat A_t
\right)
\right]
$$

### Critic Loss

The critic is trained separately to predict a value target $\hat V_t$.

A standard squared-error objective is

$$
\mathcal{L}_{V}(\phi)
=
\frac{1}{2}
\mathbb{E}_t
\left[
\left(
V_\phi(S_t)
-
\hat V_t
\right)^2
\right]
$$

When GAE is used, a corresponding value target can be formed as

$$
\hat V_t
=
V_{\phi_{\mathrm{old}}}(S_t)
+
\hat A_t^{\mathrm{GAE}}
$$

which corresponds to the associated $\lambda$-return when the same rollout critic is used.

### Multiple Optimisation Epochs

The same batch can be used for several optimisation epochs, with $r_t(\theta)$ continuously measuring how far the current policy has moved from the policy that generated the data.

Without a constraint to the surrogate objective, repeatedly optimising from same batch could push the policy increasingly far from $\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}$.

The clipped objective reduces this incentive, by making favourable changes beyond the clipped region cease to improve the corresponding sampled objective terms.

After optimisation is complete, the updated policy becomes the rollout policy for the next batch: $ \theta_{\mathrm{old}} \leftarrow \theta $

---

## PPO Algorithm Summary

For each rollout batch:

1. Collect trajectories using $\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}$
2. Using GAE, compute $\hat A_t$ and estimate $V_\phi(S_t)$  
3. Store $\pi_{\theta_{\mathrm{old}}}(A_t\mid S_t)$
4. Optimise the clipped PPO actor objective for several epochs
5. Optimise the critic squared-error objective using the GAE value target
6. Set $\theta_{\mathrm{old}}\leftarrow\theta$ and collect a new batch
